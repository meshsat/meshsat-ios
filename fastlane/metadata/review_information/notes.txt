MeshSat is a gateway app for off-grid messaging: no account, no login, no registration, no in-app purchase, no paid content. Answers to the 2.1 requests (26 Sep and 6 Oct 2026):

DEMO VIDEO WITH THE HARDWARE: attached here and to our reply (1:29; waits cut short, the phone's clock shows real time). Recorded 6 Oct 2026 on an iPhone 11 Pro Max, iOS 27.0, this build; left: photos of the node next to the phone, right: the phone's screen. It starts on the home screen with the node unpaired, launches the app, then Setup > Your MeshSat node > Scan for Meshtastic devices, a tap on node FLNR_074c, iOS's Bluetooth pairing code prompt and the connection (node id, battery, the five mesh nodes it hears). About 12 s after a new bond the node refreshes its service table and the link drops once; the app reconnects by itself. Then the Satellite page with the node's Iridium 9603 modem (model, signal, health), Home with every route, a text sent to everyone on the mesh, Map, People, and the node's live log streamed over Bluetooth.

THE HARDWARE: the MeshSat node is open hardware, a LILYGO T-Beam Supreme (Meshtastic LoRa radio) with a RockBLOCK 9603 Iridium modem in a pocket case; parts list, wiring, enclosure files and build instructions: https://github.com/meshsat/meshsat-fieldkit (compact kit). Firmware: https://github.com/meshsat/meshsat-firmware. Anyone can build one; we sell nothing. The LoRa and Iridium radios are in the node, the app only talks to it over Bluetooth LE.

PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE: MeshSat turns an iPhone into a gateway for messaging without mobile network or internet. Paired with the node it sends and receives short text over a LoRa mesh and by Iridium satellite, predicts satellite passes, shows an offline map and sends an SOS on every route it has. Audience: hikers, sailors, remote workers, radio amateurs, volunteer emergency and search-and-rescue groups.

SETUP AND ACCESS: install and open; no credentials, no sample files. Without a node: Home lists each route and what it needs; Passes predicts Iridium passes with no network; the map works offline; Messages, People and Setup all open; Setup > Safety > "Test the alarm" runs the SOS flow without contacting anyone. With a node: Setup > Your MeshSat node > Continue (iOS asks for Bluetooth, first time only) > Scan for Meshtastic devices > tap the node > the node's Bluetooth code; any plain Meshtastic radio pairs the same way. The optional Hub is set up by scanning a QR code from a MeshSat Hub the user runs; not needed for review. Text messages: iOS does not let an app send or read SMS, so MeshSat opens the Messages composer with the text filled in and the user taps Send.

EXTERNAL SERVICES: none required and none operated by us for the app. Optional, chosen by the user: the user's own MeshSat Hub (open source, self-hosted, MQTT with a client certificate); OpenStreetMap tiles (ODbL); Iridium SBD through the node's modem and the user's airtime provider; an APRS-IS server or a KISS TNC; CelesTrak orbit data, bundled when offline. No authentication service, payment processor, analytics, advertising or AI service; the text compressor is a small ONNX model in the app.

REGIONAL DIFFERENCES: none. Mainland China is excluded only for the ICP filing requirement.

REGULATED INDUSTRY / PROTECTED MATERIAL: not applicable. Third-party material: OpenStreetMap tiles (ODbL, attributed) and the Meshtastic protocol definitions (GPL-3.0). The app is GPL-3.0: https://github.com/meshsat/meshsat-ios. Docs: https://docs.meshsat.net/ios/. Privacy: https://meshsat.net/privacy/.

BACKGROUND LOCATION (2.5.4): this build no longer declares the location background mode. Background work is tied to Bluetooth events from the node and to two scheduled background windows; in the background the position comes from the significant-change service.
