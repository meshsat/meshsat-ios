MeshSat is a gateway app for off-grid messaging. It has no account, no login, no registration, no in-app purchase and no paid content. Answers to the 2.1 information request (also sent as a reply):

1. RECORDING: the attached video (about 5.5 minutes) was captured on an iPhone 11 Pro Max running iOS 27.0 with build 21, the submitted build. It starts on the home screen, launches the app and walks the typical flow: Home, Messages, Map (the map area is blurred because it shows the tester's home), People, Setup with its sections (node, Satellite, Hub, SMS, Safety, Messaging, Maps, ham radio/TAK/Reticulum, mesh radio settings) and the Advanced tools (routing rules, links). The MeshSat node was switched off during the recording, so the yellow banner shows what the app says when it cannot reach the node; the Hub connection is live. There is no account or paid feature to show. Messages are point-to-point radio text between devices the user chooses, like text messages; the app hosts no feed, no profiles and no public content.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE: MeshSat turns an iPhone into a gateway for messaging without mobile network or internet. Paired over Bluetooth LE with a MeshSat node (a small box with a Meshtastic LoRa radio and a RockBLOCK 9603 Iridium modem), the phone sends and receives short text over a LoRa mesh and by Iridium satellite, predicts satellite passes, shows an offline map, and sends an SOS on every route it has. Audience: hikers, sailors, remote workers, radio amateurs, volunteer emergency and search-and-rescue groups, and communities that keep a link when the network is down. The problem: an ordinary phone is useless without coverage. The value: one app for the mesh, the satellite and the safety features.

3. SETUP AND ACCESS: install and open; no credentials, no sample files. Without a node: Home lists each route and what it needs; Passes predicts Iridium passes for the phone's location with no network; the map works offline; Messages, People and Setup all open; Setup > Safety > "Test the alarm" runs the SOS flow without contacting anyone. With a node: Setup > Your MeshSat node > Scan for Meshtastic devices > Connect, then the node's Bluetooth PIN; any plain Meshtastic radio pairs the same way. The optional Hub is set up by scanning a QR code from a MeshSat Hub that the user runs; it is not needed for review. Text messages: iOS does not let an app send or read SMS, so MeshSat opens the Messages composer with the text filled in and the user taps Send.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES: none are required and none are operated by us for the app. Optional, chosen and configured by the user: the user's own MeshSat Hub (open source, self-hosted, MQTT with a client certificate); OpenStreetMap map tiles (ODbL); Iridium SBD through the node's RockBLOCK modem and the user's airtime provider, reached through the node, not by the app; an APRS-IS server or a KISS TNC; CelesTrak orbit data for pass prediction, fetched when online, bundled otherwise. No authentication service, no payment processor, no analytics, no advertising, no AI service: the text compressor is a small ONNX model bundled in the app and nothing leaves the phone.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES: none. The app behaves the same in every region. Mainland China is excluded from availability only because of the ICP filing requirement.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY / PROTECTED MATERIAL: not applicable. The LoRa and Iridium radios are in the node, not in the phone; the app only talks to the node over Bluetooth. Third-party material: OpenStreetMap tiles under the ODbL with attribution, and the Meshtastic protocol definitions under GPL-3.0. The app itself is GPL-3.0 and its source is public: https://github.com/meshsat/meshsat-ios. Documentation: https://docs.meshsat.net/ios/. Privacy policy: https://meshsat.net/privacy/.
